We extend our sincere thanks to **The Dart Programming Language Project** ([dart.dev](https://dart.dev)), the **Google Dart & Flutter Engineering Teams**, and the broader Dart open-source ecosystem.

- **Sound Static Typing & Null Safety**: Enabled defect-free algorithmic coordinate transforms and bezier spline dilation across thousands of glyphs.

## 📐 Donald E. Knuth & John D. Hobby — Mathematical Typography & Algorithmic Geometry

We express our profound intellectual gratitude to **Donald E. Knuth** and **John D. Hobby** for establishing typography as a rigorous mathematical discipline and pioneering the field of algorithmic letterform design:

- **Donald E. Knuth**: Creator of TeX and METAFONT, author of *The Art of Computer Programming*, and Turing Award laureate. His foundational insight—that letterforms can be described through systems of algebraic equations, pen-stroke envelopes, and parametric transformations rather than static silhouettes—inspired our comparative analysis ([METAFONT and Pocket Gull](documentation/research/METAFONT_AND_POCKETGULL_COMPARATIVE_ANALYSIS.md)) and our commitment to reproducible, code-driven type engineering.
- **John D. Hobby**: Creator of MetaPost, who formulated the seminal algorithms solving polygonal and circular pen-stroke envelope dilation and their conversion to smooth Bézier splines. His mathematical formulations remain the foundation of how computer software translates dynamic strokes into closed outline contours.

## 📚 Robin Williams, Ellen Lupton & Karen Cheng — Typographic Pedagogy & Anatomy

We honor three master educators whose seminal books guided generations of designers and shaped the architectural philosophy of Pocket Gull:

- **Robin Williams**: Author of *The Non-Designer's Type Book* and *The Mac is not a typewriter* (Peachpit Press). Robin Williams was the great democratizer of digital typography during the desktop publishing revolution, teaching millions of early computer users that a digital screen demands typographic respect—banishing typewriter habits, championing true em/en dashes, curved apostrophes, optical kerning, and visual contrast (the CRAP principles). Her generous, encouraging pedagogical voice proved that anyone could learn to see and respect the anatomy of letters.
- **Ellen Lupton**: Curator Emerita at Cooper Hewitt, Smithsonian Design Museum, and author of *Thinking with Type: A Critical Guide for Designers, Writers, Editors, & Students*. For her foundational work articulating typographic hierarchy, spatial grid systems, counter-form cadence, and framing type design as an expressive, accessible public good.
- **Karen Cheng**: Professor of Visual Communication Design at the University of Washington and author of *Designing Type*. Her exhaustive anatomical deconstruction of letterforms, systematic study of optical overshoot, stem junction thinning to prevent ink/pixel clotting, and negative space geometry provided the anatomical blueprints for Pocket Gull's humanist curves.

His leadership in developing FontLab VI and FontLab 7 redefined modern multi-master and variable font design, while his pioneering advocacy for OpenType mathematical integrity, rigorous 2-byte TrueType word boundary alignment (`loca[i] % 2 == 0`), subpixel `gasp` rendering standards, and meticulous font forensics has directly shaped PocketGull's memory-safe foundry architecture (`tool/foundry/phinney_auditor.dart`).

In PocketGull, Thomas Phinney serves not merely as a passive validator, but as our **foundry mentor**—teaching us that font engineering is a forensic science where every single byte and table offset matters to the human eye and the operating system kernel.
